Emergent Computing

We study computation that emerges from simple, local, iterated dynamical systems (cellular automata, continuous media, small Turing machines) and how to find it by search instead of designing it.

How small can a model's update rule be, if it is iterated long enough? And does that smallness bias it toward rules that hold beyond the training data?

Read the paper What is an Emergent Model?

01 — Approach

Models that compute by iterating a local rule

Most machine-learning models produce an answer in a fixed number of layers. Many algorithms work differently: they apply one simple step to a memory, repeatedly, for as long as the input requires. Systems built this way, such as Turing machines and some cellular automata, can be computationally universal with very small rules.

We treat such systems as learning models. The input is written into the state, a local rule is iterated until a halting condition holds, and the output is read back. Training searches for the initial state (the program) and, optionally, the rule. We call these Emergent Models. The questions are what they can represent, what they can be trained to do, and whether their solutions extrapolate.

03 — Publications

Papers

arXiv · cs.NE · Aug 2026

Emergent Models: Intelligence from Tiny Substrates

Giacomo Bocchese, Nicola Giacobbo, Etienne Guichard, James Wiles, Akshaj Devireddy

Wolfram Institute · Emergent Computing · Østfold University College

Defines Emergent Models, proves that some are latent-universal (any partial computable function is realised by changing only the initial state), and tests minimal instances with 20–300 parameters on arithmetic, CartPole and an adaptive foraging task. A 140-bit cellular automaton extrapolates exactly from x ≤ 30 to x ≤ 1000 on functions such as x mod 4; multiplication of two inputs was not learned.

    Two-channel continuous field (activation and memory) adapting online to food and poison phases.

  • LUTM-1

    Two-tape machine with 9 states and 144 transitions; read-only program tape; resumable GA.
